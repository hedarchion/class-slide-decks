## class-1d · 2026 · week-28 · 2026-08-11-common-illness-past-tense

# Class 1D · Week 28 · 11 August 2026

## Objective

Students will be able to name common illnesses and use simple past tense with basic signal words to describe a finished health event.

## Continuity from the previous deck

Retrieval draws directly on the prior health actions: drink water, eat soup, get rest, take medicine, check a temperature, and put on a bandage. The bridge is that those actions can be described as happening now, usually, or yesterday.

## Slide map and answers

1. Welcome and target language.
2–3. Retrieval: health actions; correct answer: drink water.
4–6. Illness vocabulary; answers: toothache, fever.
7–9. Time trail and form change: have/had, take/took, drink/drank.
10–12. Controlled tense practice; answers: B, C, A.
13–14. Interactive sentence order and signal-word sort.
15. Partner spoken practice.
16. Past tense toolbox: regular `-d/-ed` forms and irregular forms.
17. Fifteen-question interactive test. Answers: B, A, B, A, B, A, A, B, A, B, B, A, B, B, C.
18. Exit ticket; answer: had.

## Teacher prompts

- Retrieval: “What did we learn last time that can help a sick person?”
- Time trail: point to each stop; let pupils say “yesterday / now / right now.”
- Partner talk: accept fictional examples. No child has to share personal health information.
- Exit: listen for the phrase “Yesterday, I had …” and recast gently if needed.

## Likely misconceptions

- *Yesterday I have…* → model: “Yesterday I had …”
- *Yesterday I take…* → model: “Yesterday I took …”
- *I am having a cold* → for this lesson, prefer “I have a cold.”

- Previous class deck supplied by teacher: `health-steps-slides.html`.
- No external assets or claims used; emoji are native browser characters.

# Class 1D — Simple Past, Part 2

## Lesson frame

- Class: 1D (Form 1, A2 Revise)
- Date: Friday, 14 August 2026 (Week 28)
- Scheduled time: 5:20–6:20 PM; planned duration: 60 minutes
- Display: 1280 × 665, continuing the established 1D deck viewport
- Deliverable: self-contained, teacher-led HTML; no worksheet requested
- Objective: Students will be able to form simple-past negatives with `didn't + base verb` and simple-past questions with `Did + subject + base verb?`, then use both in a short exchange.
- Prior learning: The 11 August lesson introduced finished-time signals, affirmative regular and irregular past forms, and a short illness story.
- Bridge: Retrieve `took`, `drank`, and `stayed`, then contrast affirmative `played` with negative and question forms.
- Prerequisites: Recognise a finished past time; know several common base/past verb pairs.
- Likely misconception: Double-marking the past (`didn't went`, `Did you played?`) or using present helpers (`don't`, `Do`) with a finished past time.
- Assessment conditions: brief, individual formative checks using mini whiteboards and an exit response; ungraded, teacher-marked, ordinary classroom supports.

## Slide map and delivery notes

1. **Orient:** Introduce Part 2 and read the three model forms aloud.
2. **Retrieve (3–4 min):** Students write `took`, `drank`, `stayed`; ask how the third form differs.
3. **Retrieve (2 min):** Elicit the time clue `last night` before selecting `took`.
4. **Bridge (3 min):** Ask what happens to `played` after `did/didn't`; reveal only after partner talk.
5. **Model negative (4 min):** Gesture that `did` carries past time; cross out `didn't took` verbally.
6. **Guided check (3 min):** Mini whiteboards, then click the class choice. Listen for “base verb.”
7. **Correct (4 min):** Students name the exact error and explain the repair before reveal.
8. **Model questions (4 min):** Choral substitution: play, watch, visit, help.
9. **Guided build (4 min):** Invite a student to tap; non-digital fallback is numbering the words 1–4.
10. **Check (3 min):** Require justification, not only B.
11. **Apply (8 min):** Partner interview; switch after two questions. Accept true short answers and one added detail.
12. **Transfer (6 min):** Students create their own three-sentence story; invite two examples.
13. **Mixed check (8 min):** Run as many of the 10 questions as evidence requires; reset is available.
14. **Independent repair (5 min):** Students correct all three before reveal.
15. **Exit ticket (3 min):** Select A and write one original `Did you …?` question on paper/whiteboard.

## Answers

- Slide 2: took; drank; stayed
- Slide 3: B — took
- Slide 4: `played` returns to base form `play` because `did/didn't` carries past time.
- Slide 6: C — didn't go
- Slide 7: She didn't drink water yesterday.
- Slide 9: Did / you take / medicine / ?
- Slide 10: B — Did you visit your cousin?
- Slide 13: B, A, B, B, B, C, A, A, C, A
- Slide 14: I didn't play football. / Did she go home? / He didn't watch TV yesterday.
- Slide 15: A — I didn't eat breakfast.

## Misconceptions and responses

- `didn't + past verb`: Point to `did` as the past marker; return the main verb to its base form.
- `Did + past verb`: Use the same explanation; contrast `You played` with `Did you play?`.
- `don't/doesn't` for yesterday: Return to the finished-time clue and replace the present helper with `didn't`.
- Short answer `Yes, I played`: Acknowledge meaning, then model the expected short answer `Yes, I did.`
- `was/were` confusion: This lesson deliberately uses lexical verbs. Note that `be` forms questions and negatives differently and is outside today's objective.

## Grammar grounding

Private canonical reference: `grammar for english teachers.md.md`.

- `15 The past > Key considerations`, lines 9320–9330: learners especially need practice with question and negative forms and irregular past forms; narrative contexts offer useful practice.
- `15 The past > Past simple > Form`, lines 9334–9346: affirmative lexical verbs use the past form; questions use `did + subject + base form`; negatives use `didn't + base form`.
- `Event and state > Auxiliary verbs > Question and negative forms`, lines 4968–5000: past-simple questions and negatives introduce `did/didn't` because affirmative past simple has no auxiliary.
- `Choosing between tenses > Speaking and writing > Regular and irregular forms`, lines 9694–9710: a typical learner error is using the past form after `did`, e.g. `Did you wrote …?`.

# Class 1D — Simple Present Tense

**Date:** Friday, 14 August 2026, 5:20–6:20 PM  
**Objective:** Students use the simple present to describe routines and facts, including third-person singular forms and basic do/does questions and negatives.

## Slide map and answers

1. Welcome; open the private name picker in a separate, unprojected tab if needed.
2. Retrieval: B — *I drank water.*
3. Bridge from yesterday to everyday time.
4. Model routine language; invite oral responses.
5. C — *I play football every Friday.*
6. Contrast base verb and third-person singular *-s*.
7. B — *walks*.
8. Highlight common *-es* and *-ies* spellings only; do not over-teach rules.
9. Model do/does questions and short answers.
10. A — *Does Amir play games?*

12. A — *She doesn’t like spicy food.*

## Anticipated misconceptions

- Students may use simple past with routine time phrases; return to the time trail.
- Students may add *-s* after *does/doesn’t*. Point to the base verb in slides 9 and 11.
- Students may omit the third-person *-s* in affirmative statements. Contrast “I play / she plays” orally several times.

# Class 1D · National Day Collocations

**Date/time:** Monday, 17 August 2026, 5:50–6:50 PM (30-minute digital teaching segment; remaining time may be used for worksheet practice)

**Class:** 1D, Form 1, A1–A2 / A2 Revise target

**Display:** 1280 × 665, laptop-friendly classroom display. Large controls, one dominant move per board, and no essential content below the navigation bar.

## Learning outcome

By the end of the teaching segment, students should be able to understand the 15 National Day collocations, distinguish natural from inappropriate verb–partner combinations, retrieve at least 8–10 with limited support, and recognise the phrases in a short National Day context.

## Lesson flow and teacher cues

1. **Build the scene (3 min):** ask “What can we see?” Accept Malay/isolated nouns first; click an object and elicit the action before revealing the phrase.
2. **Before the celebration (4 min):** reveal cards one at a time. After all five, hide/cover mentally and point randomly for oral recall.
3. **Phrase builder (3 min):** students choose verb and partner. Tap/click is the fallback for connecting; ask “Does this sound natural?” before confirming.
4. **During the celebration (4 min):** reveal hotspots. For “Who am I?”, expected answer is **give a patriotic speech**.
5. **Picture check (3 min):** use fingers 1/2/3 or whole-class vote before clicking an answer.
6. **Enjoying the celebration (3 min):** reveal phrase, then example sentence only when needed.
7. **Phrase machine (4 min):** test natural and deliberately incorrect combinations. Explain that collocations are words that often partner together; a plausible meaning is not enough.
8. **Memory wall (3 min):** 5-second oral recall. Use picture-only tiles for most learners; teacher may reveal earlier for support.
9. **Story (2 min):** students tap five or more phrases; the counter is formative, not a score.
10. **Final recall (1–2 min):** partner recall; reveal all 15 only after the attempt.

## Collocation groups

- **Before/patriotic scene:** decorate the school; raise the national flag; wave the Jalur Gemilang; wear traditional clothes; gather at the school field.
- **Activities:** sing patriotic songs; march in a parade; perform a traditional dance; give a patriotic speech; take part in a competition.
- **Enjoying:** watch the parade; cheer for the performers; take photos together; enjoy the celebration; celebrate National Day.

## Likely misconceptions

- *sing the Jalur Gemilang*, *wear a patriotic speech*, and *take a traditional dance*: students may choose a verb that makes loose sense but is not the usual partner.
- *raise* and *wave*: use the movement/picture contrast; raise = move up, wave = move from side to side.
- *give a patriotic speech*: “give” here partners with a speech, not songs or clothes.

## Source grounding

Private grammar reference: `grammar for english teachers.md.md`, Chapter 9, **Combining words → Fixed expressions → Collocation**, lines 5605–5608: collocation describes habitual partnering, including restricted verb–noun combinations. Also **Typical difficulties for learners → Collocation**, lines 5780–5791: learners may make combinations that are understandable but not idiomatic. The deck translates this into the learner-facing phrase-machine test; it does not reproduce the source text.

It samples matching, picture/context selection, error repair, contextual recognition, and uncued recall. It is an unpiloted classroom worksheet: scores guide reteaching and do not establish statistical reliability or broad mastery.

# Class 1D — Present Continuous: What Are They Doing Right Now?

**Date/time:** Tuesday, 18 August 2026, 4:50–5:50 PM (60-minute slot; ~45 minutes of digital teaching, partner talk may extend).

**Class:** 1D, Form 1, 25 students, A2 Revise target. Mixed attainment: strong scaffolding, short models, and sentence frames throughout.

**Display:** 1280 × 665, laptop-friendly classroom display, continuing the National Day collocations deck from Monday 17 August.

**Continuity:** Continues Monday's National Day collocations deck (same word partners: sing patriotic songs, wave the Jalur Gemilang, march in a parade, perform a traditional dance, cheer for the performers) and Friday's simple present deck (routines, third-person -s, do/does). The bridge: word partners + simple present routines → present continuous for actions happening now.

## Learning outcome

By the end of the teaching segment, students should be able to:
- recognise the present continuous form **am / is / are + verb-ing**;
- choose the correct *be* form for I / he-she-it / you-we-they;
- change common verbs into -ing forms (add -ing; drop silent e; double final consonant);
- use the present continuous to describe actions happening now, and choose it over the simple present when the time clue signals *now*.

## Slide map and answers

1. **Welcome** — orient: word partners yesterday → actions now today. Model: *Look! The choir is singing patriotic songs.*
2. **Word partners** (retrieval from Monday) — natural pair: **A. sing patriotic songs**. Distractors: *sing the Jalur Gemilang*, *wear patriotic songs*.
3. **Routine or now** (retrieval from Friday) — routine: **A. She sings every Friday.** Bridge: *now* needs a new form.
4. **The -ing machine** (model) — [am / is / are] + verb + -ing. Tap a person: I → am; She/He/It → is; You/We/They → are, each with an example from the celebration.
5. **Spelling help** — sing → singing; wave → waving (add -ing); dance → dancing; take → taking (drop the e); run → running (double the last letter).
6. **Choose the be** — **A. is**: *She is waving the flag.* (one person → is)
7. **Spot the error** — fix *They is marching in the parade.* → **B. They are marching in the parade.** (they → are; verb keeps -ing)
8. **What are they doing?** (see → say → reveal) — choir → *is singing patriotic songs*; dancer → *is performing a traditional dance*; student → *is waving the Jalur Gemilang*; marchers → *are marching in a parade*; crowd → *is cheering for the performers*.
9. **Routine or now?** (check) — *Listen! Look at the stage!* → **B. The choir is singing patriotic songs.** A is for routines.
10. **Complete the sentence** — **B. are dancing**: children = they → are; dance → drop e → dancing.
11. **Partner talk** (apply) — frames: I am / He-She is / We are / They are + -ing. Say three true sentences about the class.
12. **Exit ticket** — *Listen! The students ______ patriotic songs.* → **C. are singing**.

## Likely misconceptions

- Wrong *be* form: *She are waving*, *They is marching*. Contrast the subject → be pairing in slides 4 and 6; listen for it in partner talk.
- Dropping -ing: *They are march*. Point to the formula: be + verb + **-ing**.
- Adding -ing without the spelling change: *danceing*, *takeing*, *runing*. Use the spelling cards; *dance → dancing* and *run → running* are the highest-value examples.
- Simple present for now: *The choir sings* when the clue is *Listen!/now*. Slide 9 explicitly contrasts; ask "Is it happening now or every day?" before choosing.
- Source note: learners more often use the simple present where the continuous is appropriate than the reverse (see Typical difficulties below). Do not over-teach stative-verb exceptions at this level.

## Source grounding

Private grammar reference: `grammar for english teachers.md.md`.

- Chapter 14, **The present → Present continuous** (line 8415) and **The present → Form** (lines 8419–8428): formed with a present-tense form of *to be* (am, is, are) plus an -ing verb form; be is frequently contracted.
- Chapter 14, **The present → When do we use the present continuous? → Main use: temporary events and actions** (lines 8431–8454): the continuous describes something in progress, begun and not finished, at a limited period that includes the moment of speaking.
- Chapter 14, **The present → Things happening now** (lines 8492–8499): the "happening now" rule of thumb is a scaffold, not the whole story; the deck keeps it level-appropriate for A2 Revise without requiring students to tag *now* onto every sentence.
- Chapter 14, **Typical difficulties for learners → Speaking and writing → Choosing between present simple and continuous** (lines 8526–8539): learners more often use the simple present when the continuous is appropriate; slides 3, 9, and the exit ticket address this directly.
- Spelling of -ing forms (lines 7702–7729): leave out silent final e before -ing (*live → living*); double the final consonant after a single vowel + single consonant (*pat → patting*); otherwise just add -ing.
