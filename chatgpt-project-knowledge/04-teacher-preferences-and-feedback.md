# Teacher preferences and learned feedback

## Durable preferences

- Minimize clarification questions. Infer recoverable class, date, schedule, continuity, viewport, and formative-assessment defaults from available evidence.
- Treat comments on outputs as improvement evidence. Apply revisions and retain genuinely durable preferences without waiting for a separate instruction.
- Use the full Markdown grammar reference directly as the canonical source.
- Worksheets should be ink-saving and coherent: earlier tasks scaffold the final application; title and Name/Class/Date appear only on page 1; avoid excess response lines and repeated text.
- Support the MacBook and smartboard display profiles described in the delivery-context file.

## Reusable lessons from artifact feedback

- A slide title, outcome, and reminder must not compete visually. Establish one clear hierarchy.
- Write learning outcomes as concrete student actions with examples and explicit evidence language, not vague or overly simplified slogans.
- Preserve clear outcomes during technical fixes; a repair is incomplete if important instructional content disappears.
- Matching activities need explicit validation and explanatory feedback.
- Keyboard navigation must not override focused buttons, selects, or other controls.
- Memory and retrieval activities should separate the attempt from teacher-controlled answer reveal.
- Pupil-facing text must be large enough for the target display.
- Reset should clear the current activity without unexpectedly restarting the whole deck.
- Redesigning a lesson sequence may be a one-off need; do not automatically universalize its exact number of slides or story structure.

## Feedback handling

Classify new evidence as a durable preference, artifact defect, instructional outcome, one-off request, or policy boundary. Apply current-artifact corrections immediately. Promote a reusable rule when the teacher explicitly generalizes it, when the same pattern appears independently more than once, or when correctness, privacy, accessibility, or scoring requires a guardrail. Never record student names, individual scores, or sensitive narratives in feedback notes.
